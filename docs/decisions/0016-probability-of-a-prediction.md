# 0016. How sure the speaker was: a principle, not a word table

- Date: 2026-11-23
- Decided by: the founder, in chat ("不要用词表", "灵活打分", "做成一个可复用的原则")
- Recorded by: coordinator. Principles: 3 (one ruler), 5 (keep it simple), 1.

## Principle

Every prediction carries the probability the speaker expressed, because a forecast said "for certain" and one said
"maybe" do not weigh the same when they miss.

1. **His own number first.** If the speaker states a probability or odds, that is the probability.
2. **Otherwise, read it as a careful reader would.** The whole passage counts — the wording, the tone, what is said
   just before and after, and how firmly he repeats it — not single words looked up in a list. Said as plain fact
   means near certain.
3. **Say why.** Every probability carries one short reason, so anyone can check it and disagree with it.
4. **Same reading for everyone** (principle 3): the reasons are what make the readings comparable across speakers;
   reviewers send back a reading that does not match its reason.

Scoring then uses these probabilities (a confident miss costs more than a cautious one), shown separately by how far
ahead the prediction looked.

## Where it applies

- Now: the off-record prediction analysis (local extraction rules).
- The public method: added to the verdict standard as a rule with an id at the next version that adds the prediction
  rules (predictions are outside the first launch layer). A standard change means a new version and re-votes, so the
  prediction rules found in this analysis go in together, once.

## Objections and risks

- Judgment varies: the same words may get 0.75 here and 0.8 there. The reason line and the founder's review are the
  control; before a large run, a sample of similar wordings is compared and big gaps are reported.
